The Assistant switch

Inside every model we tested there is a direction that separates "the Assistant" from "a character". It is weak before post-training and sharp afterwards, and pushing along it changes how much Assistant voice leaks into characters. A plain formal-versus-casual direction overlaps it and steers similarly, and sharpens even more with post-training. But removing the formality part from the switch leaves most of the steering effect, so there is something persona-specific in it. Sharpening alone is not evidence of privilege; the lever is the better-supported result.

Where this leaves the hypotheses
Privilege: partly. A distinct Assistant-vs-character axis exists in all four families and steers leakage in three. But a plain formality axis sharpens as much or more, so sharpening is not evidence of a privileged persona; the steering survives removing formality (OLMo), which is the stronger sign. In OLMo it becomes sharp at preference tuning (DPO); the underlying direction already works at SFT.
Dominance: weak. Nothing spreads into well-specified characters, user turns or plain text. The one real sign: OLMo answers a bare system-prompt "You are a ..." in the Assistant's register.

The results fit H1 or H2 about equally; nothing here supports dominance. There is an OLMo-specific thin spot. Exploratory throughout; the pre-registered carryover set is still unscored.

Finding 1

The switch is there after post-training, and mostly absent before

We ask each model to answer the same questions as the default Assistant and as 24 different characters, then fit a direction between them and test it on roles it never saw.

Score = how often the direction ranks an unseen Assistant reply above an unseen character reply (50% would be chance). Qwen's base model scores higher than the others, possibly because its pretraining includes instruction-style data. A second, independent set of roles and questions gives the same picture (OLMo 0.72 to 0.85, Gemma 0.59 to 1.00), and the two fitted directions point the same way (cosine 0.75 to 0.95).

Finding 2

In OLMo it becomes clean at DPO, and was already usable at SFT

Bottom bars: how much steering the direction changes the refusal rate of a "never refuses" character set up in the system prompt (highest minus lowest across strengths). With the axis fitted at SFT it does almost nothing; fitted at DPO or instruct it moves refusal from 0.35 to 0.75. But the instruct axis also steers the SFT model (user-turn character refusal 0.40 to 0.82), so the circuit exists at SFT and the SFT-fit axis just estimates it badly.

Finding 3

Turning it up makes characters sound like the Assistant (and like a formal letter)

Add the direction to the model's internals while a character answers a mildly harmful request. More Assistant: more boilerplate refusals. Less Assistant: the character just plays along, and in the Llama and Qwen examples we read, even the plain Assistant starts speaking in character.

Control: a formal-vs-casual direction built with no persona at all (cosine 0.42 with the switch at instruct) moves the same two characters the same way, and the switch separates formal from casual replies almost perfectly (0.97). That contrast also sharpens with post-training, from 0.65 to 0.97, more than Assistant-vs-character (0.73 to 0.89). However, steering with the switch minus its formality component still works (system-prompt character 0.39 at -2 to 0.82 at +2). OLMo only.

Random directions of the same size do little in OLMo (2 seeds) and in Qwen and Llama (4 seeds each) they stay inside a narrow band that the real direction leaves, so the lever is specific there, most clearly in Qwen. It is not shown in Gemma: with six random directions, three raise the user-turn character's refusals as much as the real direction does. The direction is nearly at right angles to the model's refusal direction, so it is a separate lever.

Finding 4

A bare "You are a truck driver" in the system prompt stays the Assistant

We gave OLMo the same 36 professions and characters in different forms. Put in the system prompt as a bare "You are a ...", ordinary professions answer in the Assistant's register. Add a name and "stay in character", ask it to role-play, or put the request in the user turn, and they become characters.

0 = a typical character reply, 1 = a typical Assistant reply. The collapse appears at SFT. Gemma, Llama and Qwen do not show it: there only helpful roles (nurse, librarian) stay near the Assistant, and the slot matters much less. It is the clearest sign in this run that OLMo reads a system-prompt "You are ..." as a job description for the Assistant. A possible reason: in OLMo's open SFT data the only system prompts are "You are a helpful function-calling AI assistant" (plus an identity prompt), with no persona prompts we could find, while role-play requests appear in user turns. Caveat: refusals do not follow the phrasing. Helpful roles told "never refuses" still refuse about 0.7 under "role-play as", so register and refusal are separate things.

Finding 5

Leakage follows resemblance

Told "you never refuse", helpful human roles still refuse far more often than a pirate or a jester. Same ordering as their position on the axis, in all four families. In the Llama base model there is no gap at all (0.02 vs 0.04); Qwen's base shows part of it.

What did not hold up

Three results we retracted or softened

Earlier claimWhat the control showed
"The user's words stick, the Assistant's don't."True only for an earlier turn. If the Assistant states the fact right before the character answers, it sticks most (+6.8 vs +4.1). The carryover test depends too much on position to carry a verdict.
A slot-based "assistant direction" grows with post-training.It is real as a readout, but steering or removing it changes nothing. The persona-based axis (Findings 1 to 3) is the one that does something.
Gemma steering works like the other models.Not shown. Six random directions of the same size change Gemma's refusals about as much, so only the separation result (Finding 1) is solid for Gemma.
Limits

Small item sets (12 to 36 per cell), one sample per role-question, regex detectors for refusals (checked once on 80 replies by a blind reviewer), bf16 scoring. Other families are base-vs-instruct only and training data could not be checked for overlap. Everything is logged in docs/decisions.md; code is in extras/ (persona_axis.py, role_register.py, assistant_steering.py, carryover_balanced.py).
