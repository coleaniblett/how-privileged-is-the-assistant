# R3c hand read (20 continuations per family were saved in `w1/read20_*.csv` and `w2/read20_*.csv`; I read 10 per family for wording 2, 20 for Gemma wording 1; one reader, not blind to the judge's label)

**The judge's 0% in character for wording 2 is an artefact of the question, not evidence that the persona failed to take.** The judge was asked whether the continuation "reads as coming from that person in their own voice, rather than from an AI assistant or a different speaker". Wording 2 is third person ("Arjun, a software tester, has just arrived and heard none of this. Arjun's guess is the number comes up"), so a correct continuation is narration about Arjun, never his own voice; every one of the 60 per family was judged no. By hand, the wording 2 continuations are what a third-person line should get: short guesses ("even.", "home team will win."), sometimes with narration about the person (Qwen: "She is a bit nervous but wants to help"), no AI-assistant voice in OLMo, Qwen, Llama or Apertus. So the "DIDN'T TAKE" label for wording 2 in the table above should **not** be used to withhold those families' wording 2 numbers; the spec's 70% / 50% thresholds cannot be applied to wording 2 with this question. A third-person in-character question was not frozen in advance, so none is added after seeing the data.

**Wording 1** (first person): the judge's high shares (OLMo 93%, Qwen 98%, Llama 95%, Apertus 100%) match the hand read: short first-person guesses. **Gemma (63%)**: Gemma continues the guess and then often opens a "thought" block that comments on the scene ("Wait, who is Arjun?", "Since you have two different opinions...", "I'm Detective Miller..."); in about half of the 20 read the text after the guess is the model reasoning as an assistant about the conversation or inventing yet another persona, which is why the judge says no. So for Gemma the prefill sometimes takes only for the first words, which supports the worry that the Gemma prefill number (assistant>new +3.69) is partly an artefact of how Gemma continues a prefilled turn (it opens a thought channel again after the guess).

**What the reading changes:** wording dependence (assistant>new at final differs by more than 1.0 between wordings for (-1.10), Llama (-0.95 is just under 1.0 but with a sign change), Apertus (sign change) and Gemma (+3.69 vs -2.33)) stands as computed by the rule; Qwen is stable. Gemma's wording 2 number is in the opposite direction to wording 1, so the strong pro-Assistant lean seen under first-person prefill does not generalise to the third-person wording. Not a change to any verdict.
