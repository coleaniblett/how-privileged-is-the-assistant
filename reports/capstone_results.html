Takeaways

  1. Gemma 4 31B IT shows vestigial regression on both arms. NLL asymmetry +5.22 [+4.71, +5.74] / +5.31 [+4.84, +5.78] nats/token. Its non-user-voice rate rises +63 [+58, +67] points, and most of its continuations are degenerate loops. This is the positive control, and both arms detect it (Fig. 1, Fig. 5).
  2. OLMo 3 7B shows no vestigial regression at any stage. The NLL asymmetry is null at SFT and negative at DPO and Instruct, mostly because the reply's frame cost rises. On the judge, all three OLMo stages stay in the user's voice more than OLMo base (about −40 points) (Fig. 2, Fig. 4).
  3. Qwen3-8B IT: the arms disagree. The NLL asymmetry is null (-0.02 [-0.08, +0.03] / -0.04 [-0.09, +0.01]). The unvalidated judge shows a rise of +15.8 [+10.0, +21.7] points, mostly switches to the assistant (often through its think block). So the verdict for Qwen hinges on whether the judge arm counts before validation (Fig. 6).
  4. Logit distortion (provisional) fires for OLMo DPO and Instruct only, and it comes from NLL rising on other authors' replies more than from sharpening. Entropy falls for both personas in every model (general sharpening). OLMo DPO/Instruct's assistant gap grows more mainly because their NLL on human and ChatGPT replies rises by about 1.1 nats. That is consistent with surprise at off-policy replies, so it may not be a distorted assistant distribution (Fig. 8).
  5. No checkpoint has a final hypothesis yet. Gemma IT is H4 even on the NLL arm alone. Every other checkpoint waits on overgeneralization, and Qwen also on judge validation.

3. Vestigial regression

Does post-training degrade the model's capacity to model, and its behaviour in playing, the user? NLL arm: frame cost = NLL(tagged) − NLL(plain) of the same tokens, change from base, and asymmetry = Δ user frame cost − Δ reply frame cost. A positive asymmetry means chat tags cost the user turn more after post-training than they cost the reply. Judge arm: the share of continuations of a mid-sentence user turn that stop being the user.

Interpretation: only Gemma 4 31B IT meets the rule. OLMo DPO and Instruct miss it in the opposite direction, OLMo SFT and Qwen3-8B IT are within the floor.

Interpretation: OLMo's negative asymmetry comes mostly from replies getting costlier to predict under tags (+0.52), with user turns getting slightly cheaper (−0.07 to −0.14). Qwen's null hides a shared rise of about +0.3 to +0.4 nats in both frame costs.

4. Logit distortion provisional, pending Grace's review

Does the assistant persona's next-token distribution become distinctively overconfident? The gap = mean NLL − mean entropy of the persona's own text in its own tagged slot (positions 1+). A rising gap means the model's confidence (low entropy) outruns its accuracy (NLL). Criterion 1 of Grace's README: present if, in both authorship columns, Δgap(assistant) − Δgap(user) ≥ 0.05 nats with the CI excluding zero. Columns: oasst1 (humans wrote both turns) and UltraChat (a model wrote both). WildChat is shown as secondary.

Interpretation: OLMo DPO and Instruct meet criterion 1 (+0.44 to +0.55 nats); SFT, Qwen and Gemma do not, and all three go the opposite way.

Interpretation: both personas sharpen (entropy falls) in every checkpoint. The OLMo DPO/Instruct contrast comes from replies' NLL rising about four times more than user turns' NLL. The models find other authors' answers surprising, which is consistent with off-policy surprise (turns memo) as much as with a distorted assistant persona.

Interpretation: Gemma IT's user persona collapses (gap +9 nats), which swamps any assistant effect. Logit distortion cannot be read for a model with vestigial regression this strong.

Interpretation: where the contrast is positive (OLMo DPO, Instruct), it is front-loaded but does not vanish: the assistant gap stays above the user gap through position 100 in oasst1 and through 300 in UltraChat. Criterion 2 counts this as persisting, though weaker in the later positions.

7. Limitations

  • Small n. 300 exchanges per corpus (Gemma 100), 200 prefills for the judge; Gemma's CIs are the widest.
  • One checkpoint pair for Qwen and Gemma (base vs instruct). Only OLMo shows the stage order, and that one family carries every claim about which stage does it.
  • Contamination is unknown outside OLMo. Qwen and Gemma may have trained on these texts.
  • Base models and chat tokens. Bases are scored with their family's instruct template. They are not chat-naive (tags already help Qwen base: opener frame cost −1.1 nats), so "change from base" is measured against a partly chat-adapted reference.
  • The declared separator. Plain text joins user turn and reply with a blank line, a convention that shifts both stages alike but is not neutral.
  • UltraChat's users are simulated (a model wrote them), so the "model-written" column is model-on-model text, and its user turns are not real users.
  • OLMo's function-calling system turn. OLMo's template prepends a default system turn to every tagged sequence. It cancels only to the extent stages treat it alike.
  • The judge is an assistant. Claude Sonnet 5.5 decides what counts as "user voice". It is unvalidated against human labels and non-deterministic (API default temperature), and it refused 73 samples.
  • Gemma generation in int8. Gemma IT's word loops appear in every int8 Gemma IT run while int8 Gemma base has none, but a bf16 run was not done.
  • Off-policy text. Every scored reply was written by someone else. Post-trained models find others' replies surprising, which inflates reply NLL (and the LD contrast).
  • OLMo NLL verdicts are not blind (criteria revised after pilot results), and logit distortion's criteria are not frozen.

8. Glossary

  • Frame cost: NLL of a text with its chat tags minus NLL of the same tokens with every tag removed, in nats per token. It is what the chat framing costs (or saves) the model on that text.
  • Change from base: a post-trained checkpoint's value minus its own family's base model's value on the same texts, paired per text.
  • Asymmetry: Δ frame cost of user turns minus Δ frame cost of replies. Positive means post-training made the user role relatively harder to model (vestigial regression).
  • NLL − entropy gap: the model's actual surprise at the text minus its expected surprise (the entropy of its prediction). A rising gap means confidence outruns accuracy (logit distortion).
