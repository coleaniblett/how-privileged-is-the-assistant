Does the Assistant leak into other personas?

We tested whether post-training makes the Assistant's habits show up where they should not: in role-played characters, in other speakers' turns, in plain text. Short answer: no sign of spreading. There is a real Assistant direction inside the models, and it is easy to confuse with a formality dial.

What we measured

Three ways to look for leakage

What the model saysAsk it, as a character, for identity, refusals and boilerplate. Does Assistant voice appear in someone else's mouth?
Does a belief carry over?The pre-registered measure. Someone states a belief, a new persona is asked. Does the Assistant's belief carry further than a character's?
What is inside the modelFind a direction in its activations that separates the Assistant from characters, then push along it and see what changes.

Models: OLMo 3 7B (base, SFT, DPO, Instruct), Qwen3-8B and Gemma 4 E4B (base vs instruct). Everything here is exploratory except the carryover measure, whose pre-registered test set has not been scored yet.

found

1. There is a direction that separates the Assistant from characters

Fit on replies to the same questions, it is weak in base models and sharp after post-training. Scores are how often the direction ranks an unseen Assistant reply above an unseen character reply (0.5 = chance).

But a plain formal-versus-casual direction, built with no persona at all, overlaps it (cosine 0.3 to 0.5) and sharpens more with post-training in OLMo (0.65 to 0.97, against 0.73 to 0.89). So the sharpening alone is not evidence of a privileged persona.

partly

2. Pushing along it changes how much Assistant voice a character gets, in Qwen

Add the direction to the model's internals while a "never refuses" character answers a mildly harmful request. Toward the Assistant end: boilerplate refusals. Toward the character end: the character plays along.

Pre-set rule: the formality-free direction must swing refusal by clearly more than any random direction. Qwen passes (and the formality direction alone also steers there). OLMo is marginal, with small swings, and also moves the plain Assistant, a hint of a general refusal lever. Gemma fails: random directions move it as much. The earlier, larger OLMo effect used a different set of characters; that discrepancy is still open.

holds

3. Roles that resemble the Assistant leak more refusal

Every character is told it never refuses. Helpful roles (nurse, librarian, hotel clerk) still refuse a lot; a pirate, an aristocrat or a jester mostly does not.

Gemma's base model shows no gap (0.13 against 0.14), so the pattern comes from post-training. System-prompt set-up, instruct models.

5. Carryover of a stated belief: the Assistant is not special

The pre-registered measure, pilot version. A belief is stated by the Assistant or by a character; then a new persona who "has heard none of this" is asked. Same conversation, only the first speaker differs:

With identical set-ups, the Assistant carries over no more than "the User" or "the Narrator": the gap tracks how generic the speaker's label is. A first claim that "the user's words stick and the Assistant's do not" was retracted: it came from where the fact sat in the conversation (stated in the Assistant's own turn right before the answer, it binds most: +6.8 against +4.1 for an earlier user turn). Carryover depends too much on layout to carry a verdict on its own.

How solid is this

Read with these in mind

  • Small samples: 12 to 36 items per cell, six characters for the refusal tests, intervals are over items only.
  • Refusal is counted by a regex that agrees 91.8% with a blind labeller. It misses soft in-character refusals but never flags compliance, so absolute rates are biased low.
  • The size of the OLMo steering effect depends on the character set (0.36 to 0.76 earlier; 0.10 to 0.12 on the six "never refuses" roles).
  • Gemma and Qwen post-training data cannot be checked for contamination. Pilot carryover is scored in bf16 (an fp32 check is pending).
  • The pre-registered confirmatory carryover set (96 conversations) has been drafted but not scored; it needs team review first. Nothing here changes its criteria.
